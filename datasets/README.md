# Datasets

The 7 final tables. Every table is keyed on `bioguide_id` (the U.S.
Congress Bioguide ID) and is provided in two formats: CSV files in
[`csv/`](csv/) and Stata files in [`dta/`](dta/). The two folders hold the
same tables.
All of them are generated by [`replication/build_tables.py`](../replication/build_tables.py);
don't edit them by hand.

Tables 4 and 5 (full bill text) are too large for GitHub and will be
published on Harvard Dataverse (link to come).

## Columns

**Table 1: Legislators who moved from state to federal office**
One row per legislator. `bioguide_id`, `name`, `state`,
`state_leg_years` (years in the state legislature, as researched; may include
notes on chambers or districts), `federal_years` (all years in Congress, from
the official congress-legislators term records; terms less than 60 days apart
are merged, e.g. `1963-1981` or `1961-1969 (House); 1969-1987 (Senate)`),
`federal_chamber` (`House`, `Senate`, or `Both`, over the whole career)

**Table 2: Legislator demographics**
One row per legislator. `bioguide_id`, `name`,
`gender` (`Male`/`Female`, from the congress-legislators records),
`veteran` (`Yes` = any military service, including Reserves and National
Guard; `No` = biography found, no service mentioned; `Not found` = no
biography available: 4 members who arrived after 2005 and whose time in
Congress fell between Directory editions. Source: member biographies in the
official Congressional Directory, every edition 1979–2026, via GovInfo; for
the 50 members with no Directory biography (mostly those whose service since
1979 ended before 1981), the *Biographical Directory of the United States
Congress, 1774–2005*, also via GovInfo),
`party` (most recent party in Congress, from the congress-legislators records),
`race` (`Black`, `Hispanic`, `Asian or Pacific Islander`, or a combination,
from the Office of the House Historian's official lists; everyone else is
`Not on House Historian lists`)

**Table 3: Legislative effectiveness scores**
One row per score. `bioguide_id`, `name`,
`session` (federal: Congress number, e.g. `117`; state: the two-year term as
CEL labels it, e.g. `2019-2020`), `year` (first calendar year of the
session), `les` (the score), `level` (`Federal` or `State`), `chamber`
(`House`/`Senate` for federal scores, `Upper`/`Lower` for state scores).

Source: Center for Effective Lawmaking (thelawmakers.org).
- Federal: LES Classic (LES 1.0), 93rd–118th Congress (1973–2024), every
  Congress each person served in (including before 1979). Members first
  seated in 2025 have no federal score yet. CEL's files have no score for
  Sheila Frahm (Senate, June–Nov 1996) or Charles H. Wilson (D-CA, 1963–1981).
- State: State Legislative Effectiveness Scores (SLES) through 2025. CEL's
  coverage starts between 1989 and 2007 depending on the state, so 569 people
  whose state service ended earlier have no state scores. CEL's state file has
  no Bioguide IDs; scores were linked by state, last name, first name, and
  years (see `replication/scripts/effectiveness/match_sles.py`), and 470
  people matched. Two people whose state service began in 2025, after they
  left Congress (Jerry McNerney, Joe Sempolinski), have no state scores yet.
  Reviewed matches and corrections (e.g. a CEL term recorded under Shelley
  Moore Capito's ID while she was in the U.S. Senate) are listed in
  `replication/data/reference/sles_review.csv`.
- Both scores are scaled so the average legislator in a chamber and session
  scores 1, but they are computed separately for each body, so a state and a
  federal score are not on a common scale.

Please cite CEL's data if you use this table:
Volden, Craig, and Alan E. Wiseman. 2014. *Legislative Effectiveness in the
United States Congress: The Lawmakers*. New York: Cambridge University Press;
updated at www.thelawmakers.org. Volden, Craig, and Alan E. Wiseman. 2018.
"Legislative Effectiveness in the United States Senate." *Journal of
Politics* 80(2): 731–735; updated at www.thelawmakers.org. Bucchianeri,
Peter, Craig Volden, and Alan E. Wiseman. 2026. "State Legislative
Effectiveness Scores Data set"; updated at www.thelawmakers.org.

**Table 4: State bill text**
One row per state bill or resolution the person sponsored, with text
available: "Primary Sponsor" in LegiScan; "Sponsor" in CT, MS, and SD, which

sponsor roles at all (most states' sessions from about 2007 to 2012), bills
on which the person is the only listed sponsor. Co-sponsors are excluded, as
are bills from those role-less sessions with more than one listed sponsor,
since the primary sponsor can't be told apart. `bioguide_id`, `name`,
`bill_id` (state-LegiScan session ID-bill number, e.g. `OH-1145-HB144`; for
bills from the legislatures' own archives (before LegiScan's coverage),
state-session-bill number, e.g. `CA-1999-AB2027` (`X1` etc. marks
extraordinary sessions), `FL-2003A-HB23` (2003 special session A),
`VA-941-HB120` (Virginia's 1994 regular session), `OH-GA122-HB103` (Ohio's
122nd General Assembly, 1997–1998), or `IL-GA90-SB84` (Illinois's 90th General
Assembly, 1997–1998), `MI-1989-HB4001` (Michigan's 1989–1990 session), `MD-1999-SB193`
(Maryland's 1999 session), `PA-1969-HB100` (Pennsylvania's 1969–1970
session), `WV-1997-HB2234` (West Virginia's 1997 regular session), or
`WA-1991-HB1441` (Washington's 1991–92 biennium), `NC-1995-H100` (North
Carolina's 1995 session), `WI-1995-AB80` (Wisconsin's 1995–96 session), `NJ-2000-A2261` (New
Jersey's 2000–01 session), `MN-79-HF11` (Minnesota's 79th Legislature, 1995–96), or `TX-73R-HB1801`
(Texas's 73rd Legislature, regular session, 1993), `LA-97RS-HB70`
(Louisiana's 1997 regular session), or `SC-111-H3651` (South Carolina's 1995–96
session), `MO-1999-SB282` (Missouri Senate, 1999 session), `HI-1999-HB228`

(Oregon's 2007 regular session), `MS-2005-SB2004` (Mississippi's 2005 regular session),
`WY-2005-SF0143` (Wyoming's 2005 session), `SD-1997-SB111` (South Dakota's
1997 session), `ND-55-HB1263` (North Dakota's 55th Legislative Assembly, 1997),
`RI-1997-H5205` (Rhode Island's 1997 session), or `NE-96-LB621` (Nebraska's 96th
Legislature, 1999–2000)), `bill_name`, `state_session` (the state legislature's session),
`congress` (the U.S. Congress in session when the bill was introduced),
`year` (year introduced; for California 1989–1992 and South Carolina
1985–1990, year enacted), `state`,
`text_source` (`LegiScan`, `California Legislature`, `Florida Legislature`,
`Virginia General Assembly`, `Ohio General Assembly`, `Illinois General
Assembly`, `Michigan Legislature`, `Maryland General Assembly`,
`Pennsylvania General Assembly`, `West Virginia Legislature`,
`Washington Legislature`, `North Carolina General Assembly`,
`Wisconsin Legislature`, `New Jersey Legislature`, `Minnesota Legislature`,
`Texas Legislature`, `Louisiana Legislature`,
`South Carolina Legislature`, `Missouri Senate`, `Hawaii State Legislature`,
`Kentucky General Assembly`, `Arkansas General Assembly`,
`Iowa General Assembly`, `Alaska Legislature`, `New Mexico Legislature`,
`Nevada Legislature`, `Oregon Legislature`, `Mississippi Legislature`,
`Wyoming Legislature`, `South Dakota Legislature`, `North Dakota Legislative
Assembly`, `Rhode Island General Assembly`, or `Nebraska Legislature`),
`text_version` (the
version the text is: `Introduced`, or where that isn't available `Prefiled`,
`Draft`, `Amended`, `Reported`, `Engrossed`, `Adopted`, `Enrolled`, or
`Chaptered`), `bill_text`.
Sources: LegiScan API (CC BY 4.0), whose archive starts between 2007 and 2013
depending on the state; and, for California before LegiScan's coverage, the
California Legislature's own archives (public domain under Cal. Gov. Code
§ 10248.5): its database downloads for 1999–2008
(downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov) and its older website for 1993–1998
(www.leginfo.ca.gov). For California archive bills the sponsor is the
introduced version's primary author; 97 bills from 1993–1998 whose index
author did not introduce them (authorship transferred later) are excluded.
For 1989–1992 the Legislature's archives hold only the bills that became law,
in their final (chaptered) wording, so those 625 rows are enacted bills only
(`text_version` = `Chaptered`), counted when the person is the sole lead
author; filter on `text_version` to exclude them.
For Florida, 1998–2009 bills come from the Florida Senate's website
(flsenate.gov), which hosts both chambers' bills: the bill as filed, counted
when the person is its sole filer and the first member named in the text's
"By …" line (70 bills whose text names another member first are excluded;
House bills from 2003–2009 print no "By" line, so their filer comes from the
session's bill list).
For Virginia, 1994–2009 bills come from the General Assembly's legacy
Legislative Information System (legacylis.virginia.gov): each member's
"Legislation as Chief Patron" list for every session, and each bill's text as
offered; the text's "Patron--" line names the person first for all 2,213 (6 bills continued to the next year's
session are counted once, in the session they were introduced).
For Ohio, 1997–2008 bills come from the Ohio Legislature's public legislation
data service (search-prod.lis.state.oh.us, which serves legislature.ohio.gov):
every bill and resolution on which the person is a primary sponsor (joint
primary sponsors count, as in LegiScan's Ohio data), with its "As Introduced"
text; the sponsor lines at the top of the text name the person for all 1,161.
Many 2005–2008 resolutions were published only as adopted, so 538 rows are in
their adopted wording (`text_version` = `Adopted`; one bill is `Reported`),
and 442 rows are honorary resolutions that list most of a chamber as primary
sponsors. Ohio's archive gives no introduction date, so `year` is the General
Assembly's first year.
For Illinois, 1997–2002 bills come from the General Assembly's legacy
"Legisnet" archive and 2003–2008 bills from ilga.gov's member pages and the
General Assembly's file repository (ftp.ilga.gov): the bills on which the
person is chief sponsor, with their introduced text, counted only when the
person introduced the bill. Illinois's leaders often file bills that a member
takes over later, so 80 bills whose status history shows they were filed by
someone else, or handed to the person by a change of chief sponsor, are
excluded.
For Michigan, 1989–2008 bills come from the Legislature's website
(legislature.mi.gov; its policy permits automated collection): each
session's bills on which the person is primary sponsor, with their
introduced text (for sessions before 1997, the Library of Michigan's scanned
copies), counted when the text's "Introduced by" line names the person first
(55 bills whose text names another member first are excluded; 48 texts print
no such line, so their sponsor comes from the Legislature's record). 31 bills
with no introduced copy online are in their first engrossed or enrolled
version (`text_version`).
For Maryland, 1996–2008 bills and joint resolutions come from the General
Assembly's archived session pages (mgaleg.maryland.gov/<year>rs/): each
member's sponsor page, counted when the person is named first among the
sponsors, with the bill as introduced (the "first reader"); its "By:" line
names the person first for all 702.
For Pennsylvania, 1969–2008 bills and resolutions come from the General
Assembly's website (palegis.us): each member's sponsored legislation for every
session, keeping the bills marked "Prime Sponsor", with the bill as introduced
(its first printer's number; scanned with a text layer for older sessions).
A bill counts when the text's "Introduced by" line names the person first:
313 are excluded, most of them 1970s Senate bills that the website credits to
the wrong senator (e.g. Edward Zemprelli's bills listed under Charles
Dougherty). Because those records are unreliable, every Senate bill of the
1971, 1973, 1975 and 1977 sessions (7,097) was read as introduced, and the
ones whose "Introduced by" line names one of our senators first were added
(581 bills).
For West Virginia, 1993–2009 bills come from the Legislature's bill status
archive (wvlegislature.gov): each member's sponsored bills for every session,
kept when the bill's history names the person lead sponsor, with the bill as
introduced; its "(By Delegates/Senators ...)" line names the person first for
all but one (excluded). Bills carried over into the second year of a
Legislature are counted once, in the year introduced.
For Washington, 1991–2008 bills, memorials and resolutions come from the
Legislature's website: its "Bills by sponsor" report (primary bills) for each
biennium, with the bill as introduced from its document archive
(lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov); the text's "By Representatives/Senators ..." line
names the person first for all 638 with text.
For North Carolina, 1989–2008 bills and resolutions come from the General
Assembly's website (ncleg.gov): its bill-text search for "Representatives
<name>" / "Senators <name>", which matches where the person is named first,
with the bill as introduced (Edition 1); a bill counts when the text's
"Sponsors:" line names the person first among the primary sponsors.
For Wisconsin, 1995–2010 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
document site (docs.legis.wisconsin.gov): each member's Author Index to
Legislation for every session, counted when the introduced text's
"Introduced by ..." line names the person first.
For New Jersey, 2000–2009 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
website (njleg.state.nj.us): its sponsor search for each session, keeping
bills on which the person is the first prime sponsor, with the bill as
introduced; a bill counts when the text's "Sponsored by:" line names the
person first.
For Minnesota, 1995–2008 bills come from the Revisor of Statutes' bill status
system (revisor.mn.gov): the bills of each member's own house on which they
are chief author, with the introduction text. Minnesota's texts print no
authors, so the chief author is the Revisor's record of it.
For Texas, 1993–2009 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
anonymous FTP site (ftp.legis.state.tx.us): each member's author report for
every session, keeping bills on which the person is the first author, with
the bill as introduced; a bill counts when the text's "By:" line names the
person first.
For Louisiana, 1997–2009 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
website (legis.la.gov): the site's author search for each member and
session, keeping instruments on which the person is the primary author,
with the original (introduced) version; one counts when that text's "BY
REPRESENTATIVE(S)/SENATOR(S)" line names the person first.
For South Carolina, 1991–2008 bills and resolutions come from the
Legislature's website (scstatehouse.gov): the sponsor search for each member
and session, keeping items on which the person is the primary sponsor, with
the earliest version the site keeps (the introduced bill, or for amended
bills a later printing, recorded in `text_version`); for 1985–1990 the site
has only the bills that became law, in their ratified form.
For Missouri, 1996–2009 Senate bills come from the Senate's website
(senate.mo.gov): each session's bill list names every bill's sponsor, with the
introduced text; where that text prints "INTRODUCED BY SENATOR ...", the
person must be named first. The House's archive is closed to crawlers, so
Missouri House service before 2009 has no rows.
For Hawaii, 1999–2008 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
public data website (data.capitol.hawaii.gov): each year's lists of measures
introduced name the introducers, and items whose first-named introducer is
the person are kept, with the introduced text (Hawaii's texts print no
introducer names).
For Kentucky, 1998–2009 bills and resolutions come from the General
Assembly's online Legislative Record (apps.legislature.ky.gov/record): items
whose record page names the person as first sponsor, with the introduced
bill document (Kentucky's texts print no sponsor).
For Arkansas, 1991–2000 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
website (arkleg.state.ar.us): the bills each member's page lists under
"Primary Sponsor", with the bill document the site keeps (the bill as filed,
or for amended bills its latest "As Engrossed" printing, recorded in
`text_version`); the document's "By:" line must name the person first.
For Iowa, 1995–2002 and 2009–2010 bills and resolutions come from the
Legislature's website (legis.iowa.gov): the bills each member sponsored, with
the Bill Book's document cut before the first amendment (for 1995–2002 a
scanned bill jacket, read through its OCR text layer); its "BY ..." line
must name the person first.
For Alaska, 1999–2008 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
BASIS system (akleg.gov/basis): each member's prime sponsorships, kept when
the person is the first-named prime sponsor, with the bill as introduced
(version A, plain text). Mary Peltola served as Mary Kapsner and Mary Nelson.
For New Mexico, 1997–2000 bills and memorials come from the Legislature's
website (nmlegis.gov): the legislation Steve Pearce sponsored, with the
introduced text, whose "INTRODUCED BY" line must name him.
For Nevada, 1997–2009 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's session
archive (archive.leg.state.nv.us): for 1997 its bill lists name each item's
primary sponsor; for 1999–2009, whose bill lists are under paths closed to
crawlers, every introduced text was read by number. Either way the introduced
text's heading must name the person first.
For Oregon, 2007–2009 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
public OData service and OLIS: measures whose first chief sponsor is the
person, with the introduced document, whose "Sponsored by" line must name
the person first.
For Mississippi, 1999–2009 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
Bill Status System (billstatus.ls.state.ms.us): each member's "Measures by
Author" page, with the introduced version, whose "By:" line must name the
person first. For Wyoming, John Barrasso's 2003–2007 Senate files come from the
Legislature's introduced texts (wyoleg.gov), read by number and kept when
"Sponsored by:" names him first.
For South Dakota, 1997–2008 bills and resolutions come from the Legislature's
public API (sdlegislature.gov/api): measures from the person's own chamber on
which they are the prime sponsor, with the introduced text, whose "Introduced
by:" line must name the person first. For North Dakota, Rick Berg's 1997–2010
House bills and resolutions come from the Legislative Branch website
(ndlegis.gov): the House journals' first readings name each measure's
introducers in order, and the introduced text's "Introduced by" line must name
him first. For Rhode Island, David Cicilline's 1997–2002 House bills and
resolutions come from the General Assembly's bill text archive
(webserver.rilegislature.gov), found through its text search and kept when
"Introduced By:" names him first.
For Nebraska, 1999–2010 bills come from the Legislature's website
(nebraskalegislature.gov): every introduced bill text was read by number and
kept when its "Introduced by" line names the person (surname and district)
first.

**Table 5: Federal bill text**
One row per sponsored bill or resolution with digital text (same bills as
Table 7). `bioguide_id`, `name`, `bill_id`, `bill_name`, `congress`, `year`,
`chamber` (chamber of origin: `House` or `Senate`), `bill_text` (plain text of
the version as introduced, or the earliest version Congress.gov has).
Source: Congress.gov bill text (Government Publishing Office). Coverage:
115,612 of 115,682 bills from the 101st Congress (1989) on (57 have no digital
text; 13 garbled 1989 scans were removed, see
`replication/data/reference/excluded_bill_texts.csv`); Congress.gov has
almost no digital text before 1989, so the 137 people whose bills all predate
1989 have no rows here (their bills are in Table 7).

**Table 7: Federal bill topics**
One row per bill or resolution sponsored (amendments excluded: Congress.gov
gives them no title or topic). `bioguide_id`, `name`,
`bill_id` (Congress-type-number, e.g. `117-HR-1234`), `bill_name` (title),
`congress`, `year` (year introduced), `topic` (Congress.gov policy area; blank
for 800 items with none; some 93rd–95th Congress items use older subject
terms), `passed` (`Yes` if it became law, including over a veto; for simple
resolutions, if agreed to; for concurrent resolutions, if agreed to by the
second chamber. Based on Congress.gov's latest recorded action).

Covers every Congress each person sponsored legislation in (93rd Congress
on). Source: Congress.gov API, fetched 2026-08 to 2026-10. For 36 prolific
sponsors, Congress.gov's item list is slightly shorter than its own reported
total (largest gap: 45 of 3,329); this is a known quirk of the API.

A bill normally has one sponsor, but Congress.gov lists two to five sponsors
for H.R. 1, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11 of the 104th Congress (the January 1995
"Contract with America" bills), so those 7 bills appear once per sponsor in
the population (19 rows here, 19 in Table 5).

## Validation

`python3 replication/validate_tables.py` checks every table: structure,
IDs, duplicates, CSV vs `.dta`, and that every federal bill was introduced
while its sponsor served in that chamber. Rows flagged and then checked by
hand are in `replication/data/reference/validation_reviewed.csv`.
